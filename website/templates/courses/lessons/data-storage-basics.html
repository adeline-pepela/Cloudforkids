Sorting cards into folders is just like how a computer organises files.

🎯 What You'll Learn
  • Explain what 'data' means in simple terms
  • Organise information into files and folders the way a computer does
🧠 The Big Idea

Data is just organised information - names, numbers, photos, even your favourite game's high score. Deep inside, computers store everything as tiny switches that are either on or off, which we write as 1s and 0s (a bit). Group enough bits together and you get a byte, enough to store one letter. Computers keep data tidy using files (a single saved item) stored inside folders (containers that group related files), just like a filing cabinet.

💡

Did You Know? A single byte stores only one letter, yet a modern phone holds many gigabytes - billions of bytes, enough room for thousands of photos.

🛠️ Try It Yourself

Give each learner 12 colourful cards with different pictures or words on them (animals, foods, numbers). Ask them to sort the cards into folders (envelopes) by category, then label each envelope. Compare results as a class - notice there are multiple 'correct' ways to organise the same data, just like people name their computer folders differently.

🌍

Real-World Connection: When you sort school notes into labelled folders on a shared family tablet, you're organising data exactly the way a school's student records system does.

🔍

Dig Deeper: Not all storage is the same. Some, like the memory inside a running app, is lightning-fast but forgets everything the moment the power goes off - that's why unsaved work disappears if your device crashes. Other storage, like a hard drive, an SD card, or cloud storage, is slower to read from but keeps your data safely even when the power is off. Computers and cloud systems mix fast 'forgetful' memory with slower 'permanent' storage, so everything feels quick to use but nothing important gets lost. The folders and labels you practised with today are the exact same idea schools, hospitals, and banks use to keep millions of records organised.

✅ Quick Check

If a computer only understands 1s and 0s, how can it store something as colourful as a photo?

Reveal a model answer

Computers turn colours, letters and sounds into numbers first, then store those numbers as patterns of 1s and 0s - and turn them back into colour when you view the photo.

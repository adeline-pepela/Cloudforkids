Topic 1: What Are Networks? Connected Computers Sharing Information

A network is a group of connected computers that can send information to each other. Think of it like a postal system: each house (computer) has an address (IP address), and mail carriers (routers) deliver messages between houses. Your school has a network - all the computers in the computer lab are connected, so they can share printers, internet, and files. Your home might have a WiFi network - your phone, computer, and other devices connect to the WiFi router, and the router connects them all together and to the internet. Kenya has millions of networks: in schools, businesses, homes, internet cafes, and hospitals. These networks are all connected to each other through the internet, creating one giant worldwide network. Without networks, you could not use WhatsApp (which connects your phone to servers), watch YouTube (which connects you to video servers), or send emails (which connects you to email servers). Networks are the foundation of everything we do online!

Key Takeaways
  • A network is computers connected together so they can share information
  • Local Area Networks (LANs) connect computers in one place like a school or home
  • The Internet is the largest network ever created - it connects billions of devices worldwide
  • Routers are devices that manage networks and direct information between computers
  • WiFi allows devices to connect to networks without cables
  • Networks make it possible for computers to share files, printers, and internet access
  • Every device on a network has an IP address - a unique number like a postal address

Topic 2: WiFi - Wireless Networks Connecting Your Devices

WiFi is a technology that lets your devices connect to a network WITHOUT cables! Instead of cables, WiFi uses invisible radio waves to send information between your device and a router. You have probably seen a WiFi router - it is a small box with antennas (those stick-out parts). When you turn on WiFi on your phone, it searches for nearby routers broadcasting their signal. You see a list of available networks (like "Home," "School," "Cafe_WiFi") and you choose one and enter the password. Your phone connects through radio waves, not cables! In Kenya, WiFi is everywhere: in homes, schools, internet cafes, restaurants, and shopping malls. Many Kenyans use WiFi to save on mobile data when using WhatsApp or YouTube. WiFi is faster than some mobile networks and it is convenient. The range of WiFi is limited - it typically works up to about 100 meters from the router. If you go too far away from a WiFi router, the signal gets weak and you might disconnect. WiFi and mobile networks (4G, 3G) are different: mobile networks come from cell towers and cover a wide area, while WiFi is local and comes from routers. Together, they give you internet connectivity almost everywhere in Kenya!

Key Takeaways
  • WiFi uses invisible radio waves to connect devices to a network without cables
  • A WiFi router broadcasts a signal that nearby devices can connect to
  • You need a WiFi password to connect to private networks (home, school, office)
  • Public WiFi (like in cafes) does not require a password but is less secure
  • WiFi has limited range - typically 100 meters from the router
  • WiFi is faster than mobile data but mobile data works anywhere with cell towers
  • In Kenya, WiFi combined with mobile networks (Safaricom, Airtel) provides internet everywhere
  • 5G and new WiFi technologies are making connections even faster and more reliable

Topic 4: IP Addresses - Every Device Has a Unique Number

Every device connected to the Internet has a unique number called an IP address. IP stands for "Internet Protocol," and it is a system for identifying and routing information across networks. An IP address is like a postal address: just as the post office uses your address to deliver mail to your house, the Internet uses IP addresses to deliver data to your device. There are two versions of IP addresses: IPv4 and IPv6. IPv4 addresses look like 192.168.1.1 (four numbers separated by dots). IPv6 addresses look more complex. There are billions of IPv4 addresses, but because so many devices exist, the world is switching to IPv6 which has even more addresses. Your home WiFi router probably has an IP address like 192.168.1.1. Every device on your home network gets its own IP address. Some IP addresses are "private" (only used within your network) and some are "public" (used on the Internet). When you visit a website, the web server sees your public IP address and knows your approximate location (usually the city where your ISP is based). This is how some websites know what language to show you or what news to display! Understanding IP addresses helps you understand how the Internet routes information and how devices communicate.

Key Takeaways
  • IP address is a unique number assigned to every device connected to the Internet
  • IP addresses work like postal addresses - they tell the Internet where to send data
  • IPv4 addresses look like: 192.168.1.1 (four numbers separated by periods)
  • Private IP addresses (like 192.168.x.x) are used within home and school networks
  • Public IP addresses are assigned by your ISP and identify your device on the Internet
  • Servers have permanent IP addresses so devices can always find them
  • Your home WiFi router assigns IP addresses to all devices in your home network
  • You can find your IP address on your device settings or by searching "What is my IP"

Quiz Time

What is a network?

Connected computers that can send information to each other - A network is a group of computers that are connected and can communicate with each other. Networks can be small (your home WiFi) or huge (the worldwide internet).

What does a router do?

Receives internet from ISP and shares it with your devices through WiFi - A router is the device in your home or school that connects to the Internet Service Provider (ISP). It creates the WiFi signal that your devices connect to, and it manages all the information flowing in and out.

Why does every device on the internet have an IP address?

So the internet knows where to send information (like a postal address) - An IP address is like a mailing address for your device. Without IP addresses, the internet would not know where to send the information you request (like a YouTube video or WhatsApp message).

What is the difference between a Local Area Network (LAN) and the Internet?

LAN connects computers in one place; the Internet connects computers worldwide - A LAN is a small network (in your home, school, or office). The Internet is the global network that connects all LANs and computers worldwide. The Internet is made up of billions of connected LANs!

In Kenya, which company provides internet (ISP) to your home?

Companies like Safaricom, Airtel, or JTL who are Kenyan ISPs - Safaricom, Airtel, and JTL are Kenya's major Internet Service Providers. They own the infrastructure that brings internet to homes and businesses across Kenya. They also provide mobile networks like 4G and WiFi.

What is WiFi?

Invisible radio waves from a router that let devices connect without cables - WiFi is a wireless technology using radio waves. A router broadcasts these waves, and your device receives them to connect to the network. This is why you see "WiFi signal bars" on your phone - they show how strong the radio signal is.

Why do some WiFi networks require a password and others do not?

Private networks (home, school) use passwords to keep unauthorized people out. Public networks are open so anyone can connect. - Private WiFi (in your home) is protected by a password so only authorized users connect. Public WiFi (in cafes) has no password so anyone nearby can use it, which is convenient but less secure.

What determines the range of a WiFi signal?

The power of the router antenna (typically 100 meters) - Router antennas broadcast radio waves that can only travel so far before they become too weak to use. Better antennas and routers have longer range, but typically 100 meters is standard for home routers.

How is WiFi different from mobile data (4G, 3G)?

WiFi comes from routers nearby; mobile data comes from cell towers and works everywhere a phone company has coverage - WiFi is local (from routers), while mobile networks use cell towers spread across regions. In Kenya, using Safaricom or Airtel mobile networks lets you connect anywhere in the network coverage area, while WiFi only works near routers.

Why should you be careful on public WiFi in Kenya internet cafes?

Public networks are not encrypted, so bad actors might see your passwords or data - Public WiFi is not password-protected and not encrypted, making it easier for hackers to intercept your data. Never send passwords or personal information on public WiFi. Use private networks or mobile data for sensitive activities.

What is the Internet?

Billions of networks connected together through cables and routers worldwide - The Internet is a global system of interconnected networks. It is not a single entity but a cooperative global infrastructure that connects schools, homes, businesses, governments, and organizations everywhere.

What does an Internet Service Provider (ISP) do?

Owns cables, towers, and infrastructure; provides internet access to homes and businesses - ISPs like Safaricom and Airtel own the physical infrastructure (cables, towers, routers) that carries internet traffic. They are the service providers that give homes, schools, and businesses internet access.

How do ISPs connect Kenya to the rest of the world?

Through undersea cables crossing the Indian Ocean and satellite connections - Undersea cables (fiber optic cables laid under the ocean) provide international bandwidth. Kenya has multiple undersea cable connections to Europe, Asia, and other continents. These cables carry the majority of international internet traffic.

What is the relationship between ISPs and the cloud?

ISPs provide the network infrastructure that the cloud depends on to deliver data to you - The cloud depends on ISPs and the Internet! Without ISPs managing network infrastructure, data could not travel from cloud servers to your devices. Cloud services (like WhatsApp, Google Photos, M-Pesa) all run on the Internet infrastructure provided by ISPs.

Why is Kenya called a major technology hub in Africa?

Kenya has multiple ISPs, undersea cable connections, and strong tech companies (iHub, Google, Microsoft offices) - Kenya is known as Africa's "Silicon Savanna" because it has modern internet infrastructure, entrepreneurial tech companies, and organizations like iHub that support innovation. Companies like Google, Microsoft, and WhatsApp have offices in Kenya.

What is an IP address?

A unique number that identifies a device on a network, like a postal address - An IP address is a numerical identifier for any device connected to a network. Just as postal workers use addresses to deliver mail, the Internet uses IP addresses to route data to the correct device.

What is the difference between private and public IP addresses?

Private IP (like 192.168.x.x) used within home networks; Public IP assigned by ISP for Internet use - Private IP addresses are used only within your local network (home or school). Public IP addresses are assigned by your ISP and identify your device on the Internet. Your router translates between the two.

Why do servers like Google have permanent IP addresses?

So devices can always find them - your device knows Google's IP address and connects to it - Servers need unchanging IP addresses so clients (your device) can reliably find them. If Google's IP address changed every day, you would not be able to connect to it. Fixed IP addresses make the Internet reliable.

How does the Internet know which device to send data to when you request a YouTube video?

It uses your IP address to route the video to your specific device - When you request a YouTube video, your device sends a request with your IP address. YouTube servers respond by sending video data to your IP address. The Internet infrastructure routes the data to the correct device using IP addresses.

Can you visit a website if you do not know its IP address?

Yes, you type the website name (domain) and DNS automatically converts it to the IP address - You do not need to memorize IP addresses! DNS (Domain Name System) converts website names (like google.com) to IP addresses automatically. Behind the scenes, google.com has an IP address, but DNS hides that complexity from you.

Explain how data travels from your phone in Nairobi to your friend's phone also in Kenya when you send a WhatsApp message

Phone -> Home WiFi/Mobile Network -> ISP (Safaricom) -> Kenya Fibre -> Undersea Cable -> WhatsApp Servers -> Friend's network -> Friend's phone - WhatsApp messages travel through multiple networks and infrastructure. Your network connects to your ISP, which routes through Kenya's infrastructure, then to WhatsApp's servers, then back through your friend's ISP to their network.

What is the role of your WiFi router at home?

Connects to your ISP and broadcasts WiFi so devices can join your home network and access the Internet - Your router receives Internet from your ISP through a cable, then broadcasts WiFi so all devices in your home can connect to the Internet. It also assigns private IP addresses to your devices.

How would you explain to someone that networks and the internet are related?

Networks are local (home, school); the Internet connects billions of networks together globally - Networks are groups of connected computers in one location. The Internet is the global system that connects all these networks together. Your school network is a network; the Internet connects your school to the world.

What does IP address 192.168.1.5 tell you about a device?

It is a private IP address on a local network (home or school) - The format 192.168.x.x is the standard private IP address range used on local networks. This tells you the device is on someone's home or school network, not directly on the Internet.

Which statement about Safaricom and Airtel is correct?

They are ISPs that own infrastructure and provide internet to Kenya - Safaricom and Airtel are major Kenyan ISPs. They own cables, towers, routers, and other infrastructure that provides internet to millions of Kenyans. They are essential to Kenya's connectivity.
